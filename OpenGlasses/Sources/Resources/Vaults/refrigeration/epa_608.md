# EPA Section 608 — Field Reference

Note: This is a field-reference summary, not legal advice. When in doubt, refer to the current regulations directly: 40 CFR part 82 subpart F (§§ 82.152, 82.156, 82.157, 82.161) and 40 CFR part 84 subpart C (§ 84.106). Checked against the eCFR text in force in October 2026; next review 2027-04.

## Who must comply

In the United States, any technician who maintains, services, repairs, or disposes of equipment that could release refrigerant into the atmosphere must hold a current EPA Section 608 certification. Certification level (Type I, II, III, or Universal) must match the equipment (§ 82.161).

| Type | Equipment |
|------|-----------|
| Type I | Small appliances (factory-sealed, ≤ 5 lb refrigerant) |
| Type II | Medium-, high- and very high-pressure appliances, except small appliances and motor-vehicle A/C |
| Type III | Low-pressure appliances |
| Universal | All of the above |

Most commercial HVAC field service requires at least Type II.

## Pressure classes

The class decides the required evacuation level. It is set by the refrigerant's liquid-phase saturation pressure at 104 °F (§ 82.152).

| Class | Saturation pressure at 104 °F | Examples named in the rule |
|-------|-------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Very high-pressure | Above 355 psia, or critical temperature below 104 °F | R-13, R-23, R-503, R-508A, R-508B |
| High-pressure | 170–355 psia | R-22, R-407A, R-407C, R-410A, R-502 |
| Medium-pressure | 45–170 psia | R-12, R-114, R-124, R-134a, R-500 |
| Low-pressure | Below 45 psia | R-11, R-113, R-123, R-245fa |

For a refrigerant not named here, confirm its class from the manufacturer's data before relying on a level below.

## Recovery requirements

Refrigerant must be recovered before opening any portion of a refrigerant circuit for service or component replacement. The rule covers ozone-depleting refrigerants and non-exempt substitutes, including HFCs (§ 82.156).

Required evacuation before opening, using recovery equipment made on or after 15 November 1993 (§ 82.156, Table 1):

| Appliance (or isolated component) | Required level |
|-----------------------------------|----------------|
| Very high-pressure | 0 in Hg vacuum |
| High-pressure, full charge under 200 lb | 0 in Hg vacuum |
| High-pressure, full charge 200 lb or more | 10 in Hg vacuum |
| Medium-pressure, full charge under 200 lb | 10 in Hg vacuum |
| Medium-pressure, full charge 200 lb or more | 15 in Hg vacuum |
| Low-pressure | 25 mm Hg absolute |

Equipment made before 15 November 1993 has lower levels (4 in Hg in place of 10 and 15). Small appliances: recover 90% of the charge with the compressor working, 80% with it not working, or evacuate to 4 in Hg vacuum.

If leaks make the Table 1 level unattainable, isolate leaking from non-leaking components, evacuate the non-leaking ones to the table level, and evacuate the leaking ones as far as possible, never above 0 psig.

Use only EPA-certified recovery equipment. Recovery cylinders must be DOT-approved and labelled with the recovered refrigerant type and certified empty weight.

## Leak repair triggers

Two rules apply, depending on the refrigerant and the full charge. Both use the same thresholds.

| Rule | Applies to |
|------|------------|
| § 82.157 | Appliances with a full charge of 50 lb or more of an ozone-depleting refrigerant (for example R-22) |
| § 84.106 (from 1 January 2026) | Appliances with a full charge of 15 lb or more of an HFC, or of a substitute with a global warming potential above 53. This includes most HFC refrigerants and blends; check § 84.64(b) for a particular refrigerant. It does **not** apply to residential and light commercial air conditioning and heat pumps, or to appliances containing only an ozone-depleting refrigerant (those stay under § 82.157) |

Repair is required when the leak rate exceeds:

- **20%** for commercial refrigeration (retail food and cold storage)
- **30%** for industrial process refrigeration
- **10%** for comfort cooling (chillers, commercial split systems, packaged rooftop units) and other covered appliances

The owner must identify and repair the leaks within 30 days of the refrigerant addition that showed the rate was exceeded (120 days if an industrial process shutdown is needed), then verify the repair. Alternatively, the owner can retrofit or retire the appliance under a plan; the rule sets that plan's contents and deadline.

The owner or operator calculates the leak rate every time refrigerant is added, except after a retrofit, a new installation, or a seasonal variance. Whoever adds or removes refrigerant must give the owner documentation of it at the end of the job (§ 84.106(b)).

An appliance that leaks 125% or more of its full charge in a calendar year must be reported to EPA.

## Recordkeeping

Maintain records of:

- Refrigerant added or recovered (date, quantity, refrigerant type, equipment serial).
- Leak inspections and repair attempts.
- Disposal manifests.

Retain records for at least 3 years.

## Field-service checklist before opening a circuit

1. Confirm refrigerant type from nameplate.
2. Confirm tech certification level covers this appliance's pressure class.
3. Confirm the full charge, which decides the evacuation level and whether a leak-repair rule applies.
4. Confirm recovery machine + cylinder are appropriate type and within annual inspection.
5. Recover refrigerant to required evacuation level.
6. Verify recovery weight against recovered cylinder weight.
7. Log recovered and added quantities to the work order, and give the owner the documentation of refrigerant added or removed.
