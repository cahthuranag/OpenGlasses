Memory and the brain

Keep the context. Ask for it later.

Save a detail, remember a place, or return to a conversation. Avenkin's memory lives on your iPhone or iPad, so useful context can come with you when you change AI models.

What it remembers

Useful details, in context.

Memory brings together several kinds of saved information. What you can recall depends on what was saved or captured and which features you enabled.

Facts

People and preferences

Save details about yourself and people you know, then ask for them when they matter.

“Remember Sarah likes hiking.”

Places

Where you left things

Remember where you parked or left an object. Location can be included when you grant permission.

“Remember my car is in lot B, level 3.”

Conversations

Return to what was said

Search saved conversations for earlier decisions and details. Meeting summaries use a captured transcript, such as ambient captions enabled before the meeting.

“What did we decide about the project last week?”

Knowledge

Notes and documents

Search available notes, documents and remembered facts together, with source references when available.

“What do I have saved about this project?”

Follow up

Keep track of commitments

Record what someone asked for or what you owe them, list open follow-ups, and mark them done.

“Remember I owe Bob a copy of the deck.”

Rewind

Catch a recent moment

With memory rewind enabled, use the opt-in rolling audio buffer to revisit recent speech. It cannot recover audio from before you enabled it.

“What did they just say?”

The brain, explained

Connections between the things you save.

The brain is Avenkin's on-device system for searching saved context and connecting related information. For example, a saved relationship can link Sarah to the company she works for, while a follow-up records the document you promised to send her.

When you ask a question, Avenkin can search across those sources and provide relevant findings to your selected AI model. Conversation recall can return source references, so you can check what an answer is based on.

The AI model generates the reply; the memory supplies context. Changing your selected model does not move your saved memory into that provider's account. Retrieved information may still be included in a request to that model.

Storage and processing

Saved on your device. Used with the AI you choose.

Memory storage and AI processing are separate choices. A cloud model may receive relevant recalled facts or transcript excerpts with your question. On-device AI options include Apple Intelligence on supported devices and compatible downloaded models running with MLX or llama.cpp. Speech recognition and spoken voice are configured separately; connected tools and exports have their own destinations.

Apple Intelligence needs no API key when its system model is available. In Avenkin it currently handles text; camera understanding and AI tool calls need another compatible model. For an entirely local conversation, also choose local speech and voice and disable internet-dependent tools.

Read the privacy policy or follow the offline setup guide.

Your control

See what is saved. Put it right.

Review and correct

Open Memory to search saved facts and inspect their sources. Correct supported entries, or ask what Avenkin has saved about a person or topic. Inferred information can be wrong.

“What do you remember about me?”

Forget a fact

Ask Avenkin to forget a saved fact, or use Memory on your device. The forget flow asks for confirmation and explains where a copy may remain.

“Forget that Sarah likes hiking.”

Check the original too

Conversations and recordings are kept separately. Forgetting a fact does not by itself erase the source conversation, an exported file or a copy already sent to a connected service.

Audio recordings have their own library; rewind is a separate temporary buffer. Neither replaces a captured meeting transcript. Enable the capture you need before the conversation starts.
